HOW TO SPOT A PHISHING MESSAGE

A phishing message pretends to be someone you trust. Learn the signs.

Daily article — November 5, 2026  |  Silent Security Vault — Domenick Arlon Hall, Mobile, AL

Phishing is a fake message that pretends to be your bank, a delivery company, or a friend. It wants you to click, or to send money, or to hand over a password. The trick is always the same: rush you, scare you, and hide who it really is.

What you need to know

  • It rushes you. 'Act now or your account closes' is a warning sign. Real companies do not threaten you by text.
  • Check who sent it. Look at the full sender name and address, not just the display name. One wrong letter means it is fake.
  • Do not click the link. Go to the real website yourself, or call the number on your card — never the number in the message.
  • It asks for a password or code. No real company asks for your password, your full card number, or a code by text or email.
  • It looks a little off. Bad spelling, strange spacing, or a logo that looks copied is a sign. Trust the feeling that something is off.
